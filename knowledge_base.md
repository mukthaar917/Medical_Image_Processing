# Chest X-ray Research Reference

## Atelectasis
Atelectasis refers to incomplete expansion or collapse of lung tissue. On chest radiography it may be associated with increased opacity, volume loss, displacement of fissures or mediastinal structures, and elevation of a hemidiaphragm. Appearance varies with extent and cause. Model output alone cannot establish the finding.

## Cardiomegaly
Cardiomegaly describes an enlarged cardiac silhouette. Apparent heart size on a radiograph is influenced by projection and positioning, especially portable AP imaging. A model signal should therefore be interpreted together with acquisition technique and clinician review.

## Consolidation and Lung Opacity
Air-space disease can produce focal or diffuse lung opacity. Consolidation is a radiographic pattern rather than a single diagnosis. Similar appearances may arise from several processes, and clinical context plus expert image interpretation are required.

## Edema
Pulmonary edema can have multiple radiographic patterns, including bilateral interstitial or air-space opacity. A chest X-ray model score is not sufficient to determine the cause, severity, or appropriate management.

## Pleural Effusion
Pleural fluid can appear as blunting of a costophrenic angle, a meniscus, or more extensive hemithorax opacity depending on patient position and volume. Small effusions may be difficult to detect on a single radiograph.

## Pneumothorax
Pneumothorax is air within the pleural space and may be visible as a pleural line with absent peripheral lung markings. Image quality, patient position, and subtle presentations can affect detection. Model output must be verified by expert review.

## Pneumonia
Pneumonia is a clinical diagnosis supported by imaging and other clinical data. Radiographic patterns may overlap with atelectasis, edema, aspiration, and other causes of opacity. An AI model score should not be treated as a definitive pneumonia diagnosis.

## Nodule and Mass
Pulmonary nodules and masses may be projected over normal structures or obscured on radiographs. Chest radiography has limited sensitivity for some lesions. Further evaluation decisions belong to qualified clinicians and should not be generated from this demo.

## Fracture
Rib or other thoracic fractures may be subtle or occult on chest radiographs. A model signal can be false-positive or false-negative and does not replace dedicated imaging or clinical assessment when indicated.

## Model limitations
Deep learning models can fail because of domain shift, acquisition technique, image quality, devices, demographic differences, label definitions, and dataset bias. Output values from different models are not necessarily calibrated clinical probabilities. External validation is required before any clinical use.

## Reporting principle
A research summary should separate observed model signals from conclusions. It should avoid invented history, measurements, laterality, severity, or treatment advice. The original image and relevant clinical context must be reviewed by a qualified clinician.
